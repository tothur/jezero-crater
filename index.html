Jezero Crater, Mars

This 78 × 78 km 3D reconstruction models the real topography of the 45 km-wide Jezero Crater on the northwestern margin of Isidis Planitia (18.35° N, 77.74° E) together with Jezero Mons, a 21 km-wide, 2 km-tall composite volcano perched on its southeastern rim.

The stratigraphic timeline traces ~3.9 billion years of peer-reviewed Martian planetary history: impact excavation into Nili Fossae bedrock; early volcanic activity and basaltic crater-floor flooding from Jezero Mons (Cuevas-Quiñones et al., Commun. Earth Environ. 2025); a closed-basin Noachian lake precipitating marginal carbonates (~−2,500 m MOLA); rising waters overtopping the eastern rim to carve the Pliva Vallis outlet spillway (−2,395 m MOLA; Fassett & Head 2005; Goudge et al. 2015); sustained river inflows through Neretva and Sava Valles prograding the Western and Northern fan-deltas with 15–40 m lake-level swings (Mangold et al., Science 2021); late-stage catastrophic flash floods transporting meter-scale boulders; and 3.4 billion years of desiccation, wind erosion, and late impacts such as Belva Crater.

All water levels, river thalwegs, and volcanic slopes are computed directly on NASA's 20 m stereo CTX terrain model. The Blue Dusk lighting mode reproduces Mars's forward-scattered blue sunsets caused by micron-scale ferric dust.
